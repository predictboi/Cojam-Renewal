# Prediction Market Landscape and Feature Benchmark (as of 2026-11-12)

Prepared for the Cojam relaunch. Every figure carries its date and a bracketed source number; sources are listed at the end. Third party trackers often disagree, so ranges are shown where they conflict, and unconfirmed items are labeled as such.

## Summary

- **The category is now mainstream and concentrated.** Kalshi raised $1B at a $40B valuation in October 2026 [1], and Polymarket was reported at about $21B in August 2026 [3][6]. Across the eight US exchanges that DeFi Rate tracks, volume was $120.7B in October 2026, and Kalshi had about 81% of it [3].
- **Sports drive volume.** Sports made up 77 to 81% of Kalshi volume from May to July 2026, and 57 to 82% of Polymarket's over the same months [3]. Parlays ("combos") are now standard [65][66].
- **Brokers and sportsbooks distribute the product.** Robinhood, Coinbase, FanDuel, DraftKings and Crypto.com/Truth Social all route event contracts through CFTC registered exchanges [11][13][15][16].
- **US law is unsettled.** Federal appeals courts are split on whether states can regulate sports event contracts [43][46], and the CFTC is suing states while it writes new rules [44][45][47].
- **Korea treats real money prediction as gambling.** On 18 August 2026 the KMCSC ordered ISPs to block Polymarket [49][50], and police are investigating Korean users [51].
- **Japan has no license path today.** Cash markets carry Penal Code gambling risk [55]. Non-redeemable points apps have grown instead, for example Miraima with about 1M monthly users (June 2026) [56].
- **Crypto native tokens performed poorly.** Opinion's OPN fell roughly 88% from its March 2026 high by June 2026 [37], and Limitless's LMTS fell about 93% within three months of launch [22]. Polymarket still has no token [86].
- **Trust in resolution is now a competitive issue.** Polymarket's UMA disputes in 2025 and 2026 drew scrutiny over whale voting and conflicts of interest [62][63][64].

### 1.3 Regulation

**United States.** In April 2026 the Third Circuit sided with Kalshi against New Jersey. The Ninth Circuit (Nevada) and the Sixth Circuit (Ohio, Tennessee) ruled for the states [43][46]. Per Rotowire, the Supreme Court has not yet taken the issue [46]. Arizona filed criminal charges against Kalshi in March 2026 [46]. The CFTC sued Arizona, Connecticut and Illinois on 2 April 2026 [44] and Utah in October 2026 [45]. Under Chairman Selig it withdrew the 2024 proposal to ban political and sports contracts and proposed Rule 40.11 changes in June 2026 [47]. In August 2026 it set out a three part roadmap for rules [48]. A New York lawsuit against Polymarket US is reported but **unverified** [6].

**South Korea.** The Korea Media and Communications Standards Commission ordered ISPs to block Polymarket on 18 August 2026. It cited the Criminal Act and the National Sports Promotion Act, and pointed to a Seoul rainfall market as evidence of Korea targeting [49][50]. It rejected Polymarket's arguments that it is non-custodial, offers no Korean language service and accepts no won [49]. Police opened a first criminal probe of users in mid 2026 [51], and reports that 26 users were booked are unverified [92]. Sports Toto is the only lawful exception [52]. Rewards with property value are also banned in games, and courts upheld that ban against P2E and NFT rewards [53][54]. **Bottom line for Korea:** staking money, crypto or a tradable token on outcomes is high risk. Free play with non-transferable points is the defensible model. Cojam's original design, depositing CT tokens on an answer [85], would likely fall on the wrong side of this line today.

**Japan.** Penal Code Art. 185 and 186 make cash or crypto staking a gambling risk for users and for operators of the front end and oracle. The FIEA does not fit broad event markets. Non-monetary models (rankings, badges, non-redeemable points) "largely avoid" gambling concerns [55]. Miraima, Poyp and gumi run points models [56][58][59]. Miraima users exchange correct predictions for gift vouchers through third party programs, which lawyers call defensible but untested [56]. Polymarket targets approval by 2030 [57], and the ruling LDP planned a study in late October 2026 [60]. The exchange Bitbank warned customers about using prediction platforms (Jun 2026) [58].

**Elsewhere.** India's 2025 online gaming law shut down opinion trading apps such as Probo [61]. More than 30 countries block or restrict Polymarket [50].

## 3. Community and social prediction (non trading)

**What works now**
- **Points and reputation at scale.** Miraima (about 1M monthly users) [56], Manifold (play money) [31] and Metaculus (track records, tournaments) [32] all keep users without cash stakes.
- **Content native distribution.** Myriad puts markets inside news articles and adds daily capped quests [24][93].
- **Social layers on trading apps.** Kalshi Social and Polymarket Squads show that discussion, following and leaderboards keep users engaged [67][68]. Moderation is required: Polymarket's feed has been criticized for spreading misinformation [68].
- **Big platforms are testing points only formats.** Meta Arena [20] and Truth Gems [17] are examples.

**How tokens are used**
- **Staking on answers.** Cojam's CT was used this way [85]. Under Korean and Japanese law this is the riskiest use [49][55].
- **Fee share and buyback.** LMTS staking claims a share of fees and funds buybacks [22], but this did not stop the price from falling.
- **Governance and resolution voting.** UMA shows that token voting can be captured by whales [62][63].
- **Conversion of points into an existing token.** Truth Gems convert to CRO [17].

**What failed, 2018 to 2022 (and after)**
- **Augur.** Friction, thin liquidity and ambiguous markets that could be gamed [77][78].
- **Voice.** Block.one spent $150M, and transactions stopped after May 2022 [82].
- **friend.tech.** Speculation-led growth, then a token collapse from $169 to $1.4 after its 2024 airdrop [80][81].
- **Meta Forecast.** A points app from 2020 that closed in 2022 [20].
- **Common causes.** Rewards worth more than the core activity, bots and farming, no lasting reason to come back, and regulatory exposure.

## 4. Token design lessons

1. **Ship the product before the token.** Polymarket and Predict.fun reached scale without one [86][41].
2. **Low float and high FDV launches lose value.** 84.7% of tokens launched in 2025 trade below their TGE value [84]. Up to 80% of airdrop recipients sold on the first day [84]. Keyrock found that managing FDV matters more than the size of the airdrop [83].
3. **Points markets set expectations the token must later meet.** Opinion points fell from $45 to $6 in pre-market trading once tokenomics were revealed, then the token fell further [38][37].
4. **Fake volume destroys trust.** Questions about Opinion's volume [36] preceded the drop in its activity and price.
5. **Fee share and buybacks need real fee revenue.** LMTS buybacks were small compared with its unlocks [22].
6. **Resolution by token vote invites capture.** Pair any voting with bonds, caps and appeals [62][64].
7. **Regulators look at substance, not structure.** Korea dismissed Polymarket's non-custodial and peer-to-peer arguments [49]. Calling a token a "utility" does not protect a stake on an outcome.

## 5. Implications for Cojam

1. **Position as a social forecasting community, not an exchange.** Compete on reputation, discussion and accuracy. Do not compete with Kalshi or Polymarket on liquidity [3].
2. **In KR and JP, run free to play with non-transferable points.** Do not let users stake the token on outcomes. This follows the Japanese legal analysis [55] and the Korean enforcement record [49][53].
3. **Keep the token outside the prediction loop.** Use it for governance, curation, creator rewards for quality questions, cosmetic or premium features and partner campaigns. Keep the token separate from points and payouts, as gumi separates prediction points from rewards [58].
4. **Geofence by jurisdiction and get KR and JP counsel opinions before launch.** Exclude the US and other blocked countries from any token rewards. Avoid Korea-specific "gambling-like" markets such as Seoul weather paired with value [49].
5. **Build reputation features first.** Calibration scores (Brier), streaks, seasonal leaderboards, badges and public track records, similar to Metaculus and Kalshi Social [32][67].
6. **Use templated user created questions with curation.** Creators propose from templates; staked curators or moderators approve; resolution rules are explicit. This mirrors the HIP-4 template model [40].
7. **Resolve with AI plus human review, not token voting.** An AI drafts the resolution with sources, a community challenge window follows, and an admin makes the final call with a public log. This avoids UMA-style capture [62][64].
8. **Make sharing the growth engine.** Embeddable question cards for KR and JP media and creators, in the style of Myriad and Decrypt [24]. Add OG image cards for X and KakaoTalk, and Squads-style group rooms [68].
9. **Offer a reference data and odds layer.** Show Polymarket and Kalshi odds next to the community forecast through their public APIs [76][72]. This gives users context without Cojam hosting real money markets.
10. **Hide wallet complexity.** Use social and email login with embedded wallets. Make the token optional and points the default.
11. **Run a disciplined token plan.** Realistic FDV, a meaningful float, deep liquidity, long vesting and no promised points-to-token rate [83][84][38]. Report activity honestly, since inflated volume backfires [36].
12. **Treat the relaunch as a trust reset.** Publish CT migration terms, the treasury, unlocks and a resolution log. Lead with the product working, as Augur's relaunch did with its oracle first [34].
